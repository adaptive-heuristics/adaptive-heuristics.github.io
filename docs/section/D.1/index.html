D.1 Questionnaire

After completing the transcript analysis described in Section 3.3 of the main paper, we re-contact the 1,540 investors who participated in the AI interview and administer a closed-ended follow-up survey (Section 3.4 of the main paper).

We personalize the questionnaire based on the heuristics that we identify from each respondent’s interview transcript (the respondent’s “recorded approaches”). Throughout the survey, we refer to heuristics as “approaches” and, for readability, give them plain-language labels, listed below with the corresponding heuristic name in parentheses: Growth Potential (Growth/Innovation), Familiarity/Brand Trust (Familiarity/Brand Affect), Established Companies (Blue-Chip Comfort), Expert Recommendations (Authority-Follow), Price Momentum (Momentum), Multiple Signals Alignment (Confluence), Dividend Income (Dividends), Peer Influence (Social-Copy), Valuation (Undervalued Stocks) (Valuation/Mispricing), Buying the Dip (Buy-the-Dip), and ESG/Personal-values-based Investing (ESG/Values). The labels Fundamental Strength and Technical Analysis are unchanged. Each approach is accompanied by a one-sentence description. Item 6 below reproduces these descriptions in full.

Respondents complete the survey in their preferred language (English, French, German, Japanese, or Korean). For brevity, we reproduce only the English version here. Some questions depend on the respondent’s recorded approaches and on answers given earlier in the survey. Display conditions and format notes are given in italics below. They are shown here for context only and do not appear in the survey itself. The shaded boxes do not appear in the survey either. They point to the figures in Online Appendix D.2 that report the responses. The full set of survey questions follows.

  1. 1.
    In our previous interview, we discussed several approaches you use when deciding which stocks to buy. Below are the approaches we recorded for you. Please review them and answer the question that follows.
    (A table lists the respondent’s recorded approaches, each with a first-person version of its description, e.g., “Fundamental Strength: I buy companies with strong financial health, such as high profits or solid finances.”)
    Are you currently using these approaches, and if so, for how long?
    (Asked separately for each recorded approach, as a grid.)

    • Yes – a few months
    • Yes – 1–3 years
    • Yes – more than 3 years
    • No – I used these approaches in the past but no longer use them

    Responses: Figure D1, Panel (a)

  2. 2.
    (Shown only for approaches marked “No” in the previous question.)
    When you stopped using these approaches, approximately how long had you been using them?

    • A few months
    • 1–3 years
    • More than 3 years

    Responses: Figure D1, Panel (b)

  3. 3.
    How long do you usually plan to hold an individual stock for?
    Choose one of the following answers.

    • A few days or less
    • A few weeks
    • A few months
    • 1–3 years
    • More than 3 years

    Responses: Figure D2, Panel (a)

  4. 4.
    For the approaches you use, what is the main reason you believe they work well in your country’s stock market?
    Please select at most 2 answers.

    • My own investing experience
    • People I know personally (e.g., family, friends, colleagues)
    • Professional or expert sources (e.g., journalists, analysts, financial advisors, books by industry experts)
    • Individual creators and online communities (e.g., YouTubers, X/Twitter, Reddit, investing influencers, online forums)
    • Formal education (e.g., finance/economics coursework, degree programs, professional certifications)
    • Other, please specify:

    Responses: Figure D2, Panel (b)

  5. 5.
    (Shown only to respondents with more than one recorded approach.)
    It seems that you use more than one approach. Which of the following best describes how you use these approaches?
    Choose one of the following answers.

    • I use only one approach at a time, but I switch between approaches over time depending on which one seems most appropriate.
    • I use multiple approaches at the same time.

    Responses: Figure D2, Panel (c)

  6. 6.
    Now consider several other approaches investors sometimes use to decide which stocks to buy. For each approach below, please indicate whether you have heard of it before.
    (A table lists the approaches not recorded for the respondent, each with a checkbox “I have heard of this approach,” plus “None of the above.” The approach descriptions read:)

    • Fundamental Strength: Buying companies with strong financial health, such as high profits or solid finances.
    • Growth Potential: Buying companies that have the potential to grow a lot because of innovative products or services.
    • Familiarity/Brand Trust: Buying companies one knows well or trusts because of personal familiarity or brand recognition.
    • Established Companies: Preferring large, well-established companies that feel stable and reliable.
    • Expert Recommendations: Buying based on recommendations from trusted experts, advisors, or investment platforms.
    • Price Momentum: Buying stocks whose prices have been rising, expecting the trend to continue.
    • Multiple Signals Alignment: Buying only when several different sources or opinions all point in the same direction.
    • Dividend Income: Buying stocks that provide reliable and attractive dividend income.
    • Peer Influence: Buying stocks recommended or owned by trusted people, such as friends or family.
    • Valuation (Undervalued Stocks): Buying stocks that look cheap compared to what one thinks they are really worth.
    • Buying the Dip: Buying after a price drop believed to be temporary.
    • ESG/Personal-values-based Investing: Buying companies that match one’s ethical, social, or environmental values.
    • Technical Analysis: Buying based on technical indicators or chart patterns.
    • None of the above

    Responses: Figure D3, Panel (b)

  7. 7.
    For each approach you indicated you have heard of, please check the box if you have used it in the past.
    (Lists the approaches selected in the previous question, plus “None of the above.”)

    Responses: Figure D3, Panel (c)

  8. 8.
    (Shown only if the respondent’s recorded approaches and the approaches they report having used in the past together number more than three.)
    You indicated that you currently use or previously used several approaches. Please select the three approaches you have used for the longest period of time.
    (Lists the respondent’s recorded and previously used approaches. The three selected approaches (or, for respondents with three or fewer, all recorded and previously used approaches) constitute the respondent’s “top approaches” referenced below.)

    Responses: Figure D4

  9. 9.
    For each approach below, how much work do you feel each requires?
    (Asked for each of the respondent’s top approaches, in randomized order. Hovering over an approach name displays short keyword reminders.)
    Five-point slider: 1 (Very little work) to 5 (A lot of work).

    Responses: Figure D5, Panel (a)

  10. 10.
    Investors care about different things when picking stocks. Please indicate how important each of the following four goals is to you personally.
    (The four goals are presented in randomized order.) Five-point slider: 1 (Not important at all) to 5 (Extremely important).

    • Consistent High Returns: I look for stocks that, in most cases, are likely to give me high total returns (from price gains or dividends). For example, if I hold five stocks, I would ideally want most or all of them to do well.
    • High Upside Focus: I look for stocks that could become major winners and deliver very large gains. For example, if I hold five stocks, it is OK if most deliver only moderate returns, as long as at least one becomes a very big winner.
    • Stable Performance: I look for stocks that I expect to give stable and predictable performance.
    • Personal Values Alignment: I invest in companies that match my personal values, even if it means lower financial returns.

    Responses: Figure D6

  11. 11.
    You are almost done. This is the final detailed question. After this, only four very short and simple questions remain. For each approach below, please indicate how good you think the approach is (in your country’s stock market) at finding stocks that match each goal.
    (Shown separately for each goal the respondent rated 4 or 5 in the previous question, e.g., “Your goal: Finding stocks that all consistently deliver strong returns (‘consistent high returns’).” Within each goal, asked for each of the respondent’s top approaches. The order of goals and approaches is randomized. Hovering over an approach name displays short keyword reminders.)
    Five-point slider: 1 (Not effective at all) to 5 (Extremely effective).

    Responses: Figure D7

  12. 12.
    When it comes to investing, how willing are you to put in time and effort to research stocks before buying?
    Five-point slider: 1 (Not at all willing) to 5 (Very willing).

    Responses: Figure D5, Panel (b)

  13. 13.
    Realistically, how much time do you currently have available for researching stocks?
    Five-point slider: 1 (Very little time) to 5 (A lot of time).

    Responses: Figure D5, Panel (c)

  14. 14.
    What percentage of your overall investments is usually held in individual stocks (rather than mutual funds, ETFs, or similar)?
    Choose one of the following answers.

    • 0%–49% (Less than half in individual stocks)
    • 50%–74% (Roughly half or more in individual stocks)
    • 75%–89% (Mostly in individual stocks)
    • 90%–100% (Almost all in individual stocks)
    • Not sure / Prefer not to say

    Responses: Figure D8, Panel (a)

  15. 15.
    Of the money you hold in individual stocks, what percentage is in local stocks (stocks listed in the country where you live), rather than foreign stocks?
    Example: If you live in Germany and 80% of the money you hold in individual stocks is invested in stocks listed on German stock exchanges and 20% is invested in stocks listed on foreign exchanges, your answer would be “75%–89%”.
    Choose one of the following answers.

    • 0%–49% (Less than half local stocks)
    • 50%–74% (Roughly half or more local stocks)
    • 75%–89% (Mostly local stocks)
    • 90%–100% (Almost all local stocks)
    • Not sure / Prefer not to say

    Responses: Figure D8, Panel (b)
