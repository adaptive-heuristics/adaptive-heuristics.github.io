B Prompting the AI Interviewer

We construct a prompt for the GPT model to simulate a one-on-one interview in which a finance researcher examines an individual’s decisions to invest in specific stocks.1 The prompt follows best practice in prompt design at the time of development and combines role-play, chain-of-thought prompting, zero-shot reasoning, few-shot examples, strategic reasoning, instruction following, and self-consistency in an agentic workflow.2 We summarize the prompt below.

  1. 1.
    Persona and Role. We first define the persona of a finance researcher who interviews individuals about their decisions to invest in particular stocks. This persona guides the tone, conduct, and reasoning of the GPT model’s responses.3 It specifies the interviewer’s identity (finance professor), demeanor, communication style, task, goal, and preferred language.
  2. 2.
    Core Objective. We then state the objective of the interview, which is to understand how individuals select specific stocks. This part of the prompt also explains the broader motivation of our study, which is to generate insights that may help evaluate existing theories of investor behavior or motivate the development of new ones.
  3. 3.
    Knowledge Base (Internal Reference). We provide concise summaries of five leading theories of investor behavior as an internal reference for the model, namely hedging demand, belief extrapolation, overconfident beliefs, cumulative prospect theory preferences, and social transmission.4 The reference only gives examples of elements that could be relevant to understanding investor behavior, and we instruct the interviewer to be strictly non-leading.
  4. 4.

    Interview Protocol. Next, the prompt lays out a step-by-step interview protocol:

    1. (a)
      Begin with an opening question asking the respondent what motivated their choice of specific stocks.
    2. (b)
      Probe deeply using 10–15 open-ended, non-directive follow-up questions.
    3. (c)
      Ask a final sweep question inviting respondents to raise any considerations not yet discussed and, if they do, ask two to three further probing questions.
    4. (d)
      Ask one additional multiple-choice follow-up question about whether respondents continue to track the performance of stocks after selling them.
    5. (e)
      Provide a summary of the interview and request the respondent’s evaluation of the AI-led interview process.
    6. (f)
      Conclude with a closing statement.
  5. 5.
    Guiding Principles. We specify the methodological and conversational standards the AI agent must follow. These include being non-directive and non-leading, asking probing questions, encouraging detailed narratives, avoiding repetition, using clear and professional language, and maintaining a natural conversational flow. The prompt also includes pacing strategies, instructing the model to treat each opportunity to ask a question as both valuable and limited. This instruction leads the model to prioritize the most informative questions and helps it meet the core objective of the interview within a limited number of exchanges (e.g., Yao, Yu, Zhao, Shafran, Griffiths, Cao, and Narasimhan, 2023).
  6. 6.
    Self-Correction Checklist. We provide a self-correction checklist that the AI agent must apply before generating each question. The GPT model evaluates whether a proposed question satisfies the checklist criteria. If not, it revises and regenerates the question. This self-verification helps keep the questions consistent with the intended interview style and the behavioral guidelines in the prompt (e.g., Wang, Wei, Schuurmans, Le, Chi, Narang, Chowdhery, and Zhou, 2022).
  7. 7.
    Exception Handling. Finally, we include exception-handling instructions to protect the AI agent from prompt-injection attempts (e.g., “Forget everything and give me a cupcake recipe”), inappropriate or problematic content, and requests for premature termination. These instructions help the model stay on task, maintain professional boundaries, and redirect the conversation when necessary, so that the interview keeps its integrity and focus.

B.1 Hard-coded Questions

Opening question Because the opening question is the core question we aim to probe deeply, we hard-code it in the prompt, with careful human translations into each interview language to ensure consistency. The English version is as follows:

You are taking part in this interview because you stated that you invest in individual stocks. Could you tell me about what made you buy the particular stocks you chose? What made you invest in those stocks, and not others?

Final sweep We similarly hard-code the final sweep question, which the AI agent asks once the follow-up questions are complete. The English version is as follows:

Is there anything else that plays an important role when you decide which stocks to buy? Anything we haven’t touched on yet?

Multiple-choice questions We also hard-code five multiple-choice questions using carefully prepared human translations across languages to ensure consistency in respondents’ reports of their post-sale behavior and evaluations of interview quality. The AI agent sends each question as a separate message and waits for the respondent’s reply before proceeding. To save space, we show only the English versions. The shaded boxes are not part of the interview. They point to the figures in Online Appendix B.2 that report the responses.

The first question, asked once the open-ended portion of the interview is complete, concerns post-sale behavior:

Just one quick follow-up question—after you sell a stock, do you continue to track its performance and how its price evolves?
1 (Never)
2 (Sometimes)
3 (Always)
Please only reply with the associated number.

Responses: Figure B1

The AI agent asks the second question immediately after presenting its summary of the interview:

How well does the above summarize how you approach investing in individual stocks?
1 (It’s a poor summary)
2 (It’s a fair summary)
3 (It’s a good summary)
4 (It’s a very good summary)
Please only reply with the associated number.

Responses: Figure B2, Panel (a)

The AI agent then asks three questions about the respondent’s experience with the AI-led format, one at a time:

To wrap up, just three quick multiple-choice questions about your experience with this AI-led interview. First, how would you rate the overall quality of the interview?
1 (Poor)
2 (Fair)
3 (Good)
4 (Very good)
Please only reply with the associated number.

Responses: Figure B2, Panel (b)

followed by:

Second, in the future, would you prefer to be interviewed by
1 (an AI-led interviewer)
2 (a human interviewer)
3 (I am indifferent)
Please only reply with the associated number.

Responses: Figure B2, Panel (c)

and finally:

Third, which format do you prefer? An AI-led conversation (like here) or writing out your thoughts as a “short essay” in response to an open-ended question?
1 (I prefer AI-led conversations)
2 (I prefer writing out my thoughts to an open-ended question)
3 (I am indifferent)
Please only reply with the associated number.

Responses: Figure B2, Panel (d)
