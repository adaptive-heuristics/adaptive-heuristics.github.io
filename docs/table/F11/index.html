Table F11: Do Adopted Heuristics Deliver? Awareness-Restricted Alternatives

This table repeats the comparisons of Tables 7 and 8 of the main paper with the alternative-heuristics benchmark restricted to approaches each investor reports being aware of in the follow-up survey. The adopted set consists of the investor’s interview-recorded heuristics among the seven covered by archival data. The row “Alternative heuristics” replaces this set with an equally sized set drawn randomly, without replacement, from covered heuristics not adopted in the interview that the investor reports having heard of in the follow-up survey. Previously used alternatives remain eligible. We evaluate both sets in the investor’s country on her prioritized objectives, defined as objectives rated strictly above her own average importance across returns, upside, and stability. The “Overall” column first averages performance across each investor’s prioritized objectives and then across investors; the other columns average across investors who prioritize the corresponding objective. Panel A uses historical performance from July 2015 through June 2025. Panel B uses performance from October through December 2025, with CAPM betas estimated over September 2015 through August 2025 and held fixed. Returns use CAPM alpha, upside uses the top-1% hit-rate spread, and stability uses the sign-reversed total return-volatility spread. We standardize performance within objective across the seventy heuristic–country pairs, separately for each panel. Each benchmark reports the mean across 10,000 draws, with the standard deviation across draws in parentheses. Bracketed one-sided p-values give the fractions of draws that perform at least as well as the adopted set. Both panels use the same 360 investors, retaining adopters of one to three covered heuristics who prioritize at least one financial objective and have enough known alternatives for an equally sized draw. The returns, upside, and stability columns include 178, 129, and 221 investors, respectively; these subsamples overlap. Statistical significance at the 10%, 5%, and 1% levels is denoted by *, **, and ***, respectively. p-values are displayed to three decimals; stars use unrounded values.
