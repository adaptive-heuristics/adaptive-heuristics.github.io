Table F13: Heuristic Adoption and Historical Effectiveness: Reduced Form

This table examines whether investors are more likely to adopt heuristics that are historically more effective, in their own country, for the objectives they consider important. It re-estimates the adoption regression of Table 6 of the main paper in reduced form, replacing perceived effectiveness ηihgP with historical effectiveness ηhgcH. Because we estimate historical effectiveness from archival data alone (Section 5.3 of the main paper), survey answers cannot affect it, and the estimates are immune to ex-post rationalization of one’s own choices. The dependent variable, D(Adoption), is a dummy variable equal to one if the heuristic is among those we identify in the interview of the investor. The key independent variable is the interaction Objective importance × Historical effectiveness, in which Objective importance is the five-point importance rating the investor gives to the objective, standardized within investor across the three financial objectives, and Historical effectiveness is the delivery of the heuristic on the objective in the country of the investor, estimated for the period running from July 2015 through June 2025, the ten years preceding our interviews, and standardized within objective across the seventy heuristic–country pairs. Because the analysis requires no effectiveness rating, the sample is the full grid of the 600 follow-up respondents, the seven heuristics covered by archival data, and the three financial objectives (12,600 observations), without any conditioning on which heuristics an investor rates. Investor FE, Investor × Objective FE, and Country × Heuristic × Objective FE refer to the corresponding fixed effects. We cluster standard errors at the country level and report them in parentheses. Given the small number of clusters (ten), we base the stars in Columns (1) and (2) on wild-cluster bootstrap p-values (Webb six-point weights, 9,999 replications). In Column (3), the bootstrap is not computable under the high-dimensional fixed effects, so we base the stars on the country-clustered standard errors. The interaction remains significant at the 1% level when we instead cluster standard errors at the investor level. Statistical significance at the 10%, 5%, and 1% levels is denoted by *, **, and ***, respectively.
