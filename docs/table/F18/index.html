Table F18: Do Adopted Heuristics Deliver? Post-Adoption Evidence

This table repeats the analysis of Table 8 of the main paper over post-adoption periods based on reported use durations. For each adopted heuristic, we use the duration of current use or, if the investor has stopped using it, the duration before stopping. We exclude investor–heuristic pairs that report a few months or an unknown duration. Taking June 30, 2026 as the common reference date, we use minimum durations of one and three years for reports of one to three years and more than three years, respectively. Monthly portfolio sorts therefore begin in July 2025 or July 2023, and we measure delivery over the month following each sort. Outcomes cover August through December 2025 (five months) or August 2023 through December 2025 (29 months, the longest period). The average period is approximately 20 months per investor, with retained heuristics weighted equally within each investor. We evaluate the choices recorded in the interviews through December 2025, including heuristics no longer used. For returns, we calculate the CAPM alpha with market betas fixed at their heuristic–country estimates from July 2013 through June 2023 portfolio sorts. For upside and stability, we use the top-1% hit-rate spread and the sign-reversed total return volatility spread. We standardize outcomes in both periods using the within-objective means and sample standard deviations across the seventy heuristic–country pairs in the 29-month window. Prioritized objectives and aggregation are otherwise as in Table 8 of the main paper. The two benchmark rows replace the retained adopted set of each investor with an equally sized random draw without replacement from (i) the covered heuristics she did not adopt in the interview (Alternative heuristics) and (ii) all seven covered heuristics (Randomly selected heuristics). The first pool also excludes adopted heuristics dropped by the duration filter. We randomly assign the drawn heuristics to the periods of the adopted heuristics and evaluate them in the same country on the same prioritized objectives. These two rows report means across 10,000 draws, with standard deviations across draws in parentheses, and their p-values are the one-sided fraction of draws that deliver at least as much as the adopted set. The sample comprises 371 investors and 621 retained investor–heuristic pairs. The columns Returns, Upside, and Stability use 180, 137, and 224 of these investors, respectively. Interpreting these results as post-adoption delivery assumes that the reported durations identify the start of use and that the reported priorities apply during the evaluation period. Statistical significance at the 10%, 5%, and 1% levels is denoted by *, **, and ***, respectively, referring to the p-values in the brackets.
