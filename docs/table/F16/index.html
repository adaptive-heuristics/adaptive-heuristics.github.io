Table F16: Do Adopted Heuristics Deliver on Non-Prioritized Objectives? Average Stock and JKP-Factor Benchmarks

This table reports two additional benchmarks for Table F12, using historical effectiveness over July 2015 through June 2025, the ten years preceding our interviews. An objective counts as non-prioritized if the investor rates it strictly below her own average across the three financial objectives. The row Adopted heuristics repeats that of Table F12, with the same sample, non-prioritized objectives, adopted heuristics, aggregation, and standardization. The row Average stock reports the delivery of random stock sorting. Its long–short performance is zero in expectation before standardization, and we standardize it with the same within-objective means and standard deviations as heuristic effectiveness. This benchmark is a constant, so it has no standard deviation. Its one-sided p-value tests whether the adopted heuristics deliver more, using a wild-cluster bootstrap by country (Webb six-point weights, 9,999 replications). The row Randomly selected JKP-factor-based portfolios replaces the adopted set of each investor with an equally sized random draw from the 153 factors of Jensen, Kelly, and Pedersen (2023) that are computable in her country, each in its published long–short direction and standardized with the same within-objective means and standard deviations as heuristic effectiveness. This row reports the mean across 10,000 draws, with the standard deviation across draws in parentheses, and its p-value is the one-sided fraction of draws that deliver at least as much as the adopted set. The sample comprises the 427 follow-up respondents of Table F12, and the columns Returns, Upside, and Stability use 184, 255, and 146 of them, respectively. Statistical significance at the 10%, 5%, and 1% levels is denoted by *, **, and ***, respectively, referring to the p-values in the brackets.
