Table F15: Do Adopted Heuristics Deliver on Non-Prioritized Objectives?

This table repeats the analysis of Table 7 of the main paper on the objectives investors do not prioritize. An objective counts as non-prioritized if the investor rates it strictly below her own average across the three financial objectives. The adopted heuristics of each investor are those we identify in her interview among the seven covered by archival data (Table 5 of the main paper). The row Adopted heuristics reports the delivery of these heuristics on the non-prioritized objectives in the country of the investor. It averages over investors, in the column Overall after first averaging the non-prioritized objectives of each investor so that each investor enters once, and in the other columns over the investors for whom that objective is non-prioritized. The design is otherwise identical to Table 7 of the main paper. The two benchmark rows replace the adopted rule set of each investor with an equally sized random draw from (i) the covered heuristics she does not adopt (Alternative heuristics) and (ii) all seven covered heuristics (Randomly selected heuristics). These two rows report means across 10,000 draws, with standard deviations across draws in parentheses, and their p-values are the one-sided fraction of draws that deliver at least as much as the adopted set. The sample comprises the 427 follow-up respondents who adopt at least one covered heuristic and have at least one non-prioritized financial objective, excluding adopters of four or more of the seven. The columns Returns, Upside, and Stability use 184, 255, and 146 of them, respectively. Table F16 reports the average-stock and JKP-factor-based portfolio benchmarks, with the same adopted-heuristics row. Statistical significance at the 10%, 5%, and 1% levels is denoted by *, **, and ***, respectively, referring to the p-values in the brackets.
