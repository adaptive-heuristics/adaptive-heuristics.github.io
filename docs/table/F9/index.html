Table F9: Heuristic Adoption: Logit Estimates

This table examines whether investors adopt the heuristics they perceive as effective for the objectives they consider important, re-estimating the linear probability models of Table 6 of the main paper as fixed-effects logit models. We estimate the following logit counterpart of equation (1) of the main paper:

Pr⁡(D(Adoption)ih=1)=Λ(β1(θig×ηihgP)+β2ηihgP+β3θig+αi),

in which Λ(⋅) denotes the logistic function, and the dependent variable, D(Adoption)ih, equals one if heuristic h is among those we identify in the interview of investor i. The key independent variable is the interaction θig×ηihgP, printed as Objective importance × Perceived effectiveness, in which θig is the five-point importance rating investor i gives to objective g, and ηihgP is the five-point rating she gives to how effective heuristic h is at finding stocks that match the objective in her own local stock market. We standardize both variables within investor, as in Table 6 of the main paper. The starting sample is the same 3,744 investor–heuristic–objective observations from 572 investors. The logit estimation drops fixed-effect groups without variation in the outcome, because the group effect fits their observations perfectly, which leaves 3,291, 3,291, and 3,181 observations in Columns (1) through (3), respectively. Coefficients are in log-odds and are therefore not directly comparable in scale to the linear probability estimates. The row Pseudo R2 reports McFadden’s pseudo-R2, computed against an intercept-only logit on the estimation sample of each column. Investor FE, Investor × Objective FE, and Country × Heuristic × Objective FE refer to the corresponding fixed effects. We cluster standard errors at the investor level and report them in parentheses. Statistical significance at the 10%, 5%, and 1% levels is denoted by *, **, and ***, respectively.
