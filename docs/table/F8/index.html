Table F8: Relationship between Perceived and Historical Effectiveness: Heterogeneity across Investor Groups

This table examines whether the relationship between perceived and historical heuristic effectiveness varies with investor characteristics. The dependent variable, Perceived effectiveness, and the key independent variable, Historical effectiveness, are as in Table F7. Column (1) reproduces Column (1) of Table F7, which pools the three financial objectives with investor and objective fixed effects. Columns (2) through (7) each add one interaction between historical effectiveness and an investor characteristic, and Column (8) includes all six interactions jointly. The six characteristics are the indicators Male, Older (age ≥35), Higher education (bachelor’s degree or above), High wealth (investable assets ≥ USD 500,000), Advanced knowledge (self-reported advanced or expert investment knowledge), and Aggressive (self-reported willingness to take above-average or substantial financial risks). The investor fixed effects absorb their main effects. The sample comprises the 2,561 investor–heuristic–objective observations from 560 investors in Column (1) of Table F7. We cluster standard errors at the country level and report them in parentheses. Given the small number of clusters (ten), we base inference on wild-cluster bootstrap p-values (Webb six-point weights, 9,999 replications). Statistical significance at the 10%, 5%, and 1% levels is denoted by *, **, and ***, respectively.
